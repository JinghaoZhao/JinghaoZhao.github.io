I · CURRENT FOCUS Google Cloud Infrastructure

Google Cloud · GCE Bare Metal

Dedicated hardware,
architected for the cloud.

On Google’s GCE Bare Metal team, I focus on how dedicated physical machines behave as first-class citizens of a cloud platform.

Making bare metal feel cloud-native means bridging hardware boundaries, automated fleet lifecycle, and predictable fabric performance across the stack.

ARCHITECTURAL SHOJI STUDY · SELECT A STRATUM
01 / PHYSICAL BOUNDARY

Host & Fabric

Dedicated silicon, high-speed networking, and storage I/O boundaries—preserving raw physical predictability while integrating seamlessly with Google Cloud services.

02 / CONTROL PLANE

Fleet Lifecycle

Automated provisioning, orchestration, and safe control-plane transitions so dedicated physical hosts can be operated with cloud-native discipline.

03 / SYSTEM GARMENT

End-to-End Behavior

Predictable latency, fault isolation, and operability at scale—treating hardware, fabric, and control software as one coherent system.

IN PRACTICE / SELECTED WORK

Google Cloud × CME Group

Ultra-low-latency trading

実 · IN PRACTICE

One place this architecture comes together is the low-latency trading platform built through the Google Cloud and CME Group collaboration, which I contribute to on the GCE Bare Metal side.

The collaboration brings dedicated bare metal compute and deterministic networking to financial markets—combining the precision of physical infrastructure with the flexibility of the cloud.

II · A LITTLE ABOUT ME Systems & Craft

Deep in the stack.
Deliberate on the surface.

I care as much about how a system behaves under the hood as how an interface feels in someone’s hands.

View my CV ↗ Google Scholar ↗ Connect on LinkedIn ↗

Before Google, I received my Ph.D. in Computer Science from the University of California, Los Angeles, advised by Prof. Songwu Lu, and my B.S. in Electronic Engineering from Shanghai Jiao Tong University. My research spans networked systems, mobile security, and edge and cloud infrastructure—tracing how real systems fail, recover, and perform.

Alongside systems engineering, I’m drawn to product interaction and visual aesthetics: clear hierarchy, purposeful motion, and interfaces that make complex technical ideas feel effortless.
